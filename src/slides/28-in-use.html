Don't get hung up on perceptual

The two squares look like the same color. They are not — slide them together. Each surround pushes its square away from itself, and the two pushes happen to cancel: two colors, one appearance. Simultaneous contrast — Albers' four-that-look-like-three. And it is only one of many. Colors don't exist in a vacuum. They shift with their neighbours, their size, their edges. Every number in this talk was measured on swatches apart, on white; a perceptual model gets each color right and says nothing about the rest. Explore the other effects at colorandcontrast.com.
